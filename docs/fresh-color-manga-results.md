# Fresh color and monochrome manga trials

Five Japanese pages were compared with a frozen checkout of `c270f0c`. The color set comprises the chapter 7 cover of *Ubunchu!* and pages 4 and 5 of *Pepper&Carrot* episode 6. Two monochrome *Ubunchu!* pages cover a dense spreadsheet (chapter 2, page 10) and a vertically lettered conversation (chapter 5, page 10). Trials used the Japanese page pixels, Paddle v6 medium, Hayai with reader presentations, TranslateGemma 27B, coverage-first translation, localized cleanup, and always-show aggressive replacement. Source hashes and runtime options are in each experiment's `plan.json`.

## Reading and rendering

- On the cover, fitting the crop to the shape of the display lettering recovered “Ubunchu!” from the oversized title. The baseline said “Oops!” and left the first orange character intact. Extending an erase mask using nearby matching ink and allowing separate placement inside an already cleaned title footprint also restored the translated school-romance caption. The English title is still much smaller than the original. The unrecognized Japanese author credit has a printed Latin counterpart immediately below it.
- On the monochrome dialogue page, two adjacent groups form one concessive sentence. Reading the continuation turned “How about this CD?” and “It has absolutely nothing to do with it.” into “It doesn't matter at all what this CD is like.” A group can join only when its geometry, the white space and its Japanese clause ending support the continuation.
- On the spreadsheet page, reflowing upright letters before reading the vertical caption recovered the printed acronym CUI. High-confidence printed Latin labels retain their original lettering when the bound translation repeats their words and only adds editorial punctuation. This preserved 17 labels on the initial spreadsheet rerender. Using the printed horizontal rules separated five independent Japanese cells that the baseline rewrote as “From elementary school, I wasn't friends with anyone, and I was always alone in class.” Their separate translations now include “From elementary school,” “With anyone,” and “class.” The short cells lack question context, so the intended senses of some fragments remain uncertain.
- On the painted pages, a rectified crop and its two disjoint halves provided corroborating readings of パチ. With quoted same-page Japanese dialogue, the translator could distinguish applause at the contest from crackling in a separate fire control. Context is scoped to repeated katakana sound effects in the specialist crop-reader pipeline. Native OCR membership and the exact translation target remain bound to the result. Explicit isolated translation is available through `--translation-context isolated`.
- Gemma 3 12B and Qwen2.5-VL 7B were tested on image crops. The Gemma trial recovered the names Shichimi and Coriander in some contexts, yet retained Japanese or romanized the sound in others, and reversed or introduced a question on dialogue examples. The vision outputs remain comparison evidence.

## GPU reconstruction trials

SDXL Inpainting 0.1 ran on the RTX 5090 with 25 configured steps and a peak PyTorch allocation of 8.96 GiB. Six real lettering regions were visually reviewed. For eight additional masks, the original, known artwork under each mask made a pixel comparison possible. The best tested SDXL strength (0.60) gave a mean absolute RGB error of 22.76 on the 0–255 scale. Single-view LaMa scored 18.76 and two-view LaMa scored 17.68 on those same eight masks. SDXL beat two-view LaMa on two of them. SDXL added faint lettering to an empty balloon and blurred spreadsheet rules. LaMa remains the default learned repair in this configuration. [SDXL model card](https://huggingface.co/diffusers/stable-diffusion-xl-1.0-inpainting-0.1)

A separate trial used 28 artificial masks on seven pages. Combining native and horizontally reflected LaMa predictions reduced average error from 18.40 to 17.55 and improved 25 of 28 crops. On the 16 monochrome crops, error fell from 21.64 to 20.51. Four views reached 17.10 across all crops but softened some real ink lines. Two views are used for monochrome as well as color. Reconstruction is committed only inside the selected ink mask.

## Local evidence and sources

Under `test-results/production-best-effort-v8/`, `fresh-base-verified/` holds the baseline, `fresh-final-review/` holds comparison images and pixel audit, and `fresh-sdxl-{actual,controls}/` and `fresh-mirror/` hold the reconstruction experiments. On P04, five detected crowd clusters previously yielded phrases including “Excellent craftsmanship.” All five rendered as clapping with scene context. On P05 the largest lower-right effect previously yielded “Using a waist warmer makes your waist feel good.” The final output reads as repeated applause and preserves more red fabric and green background. The lower garment is still visibly softened. Two effects in the upper panel remain mistranslated, and some decorative lettering falls outside the detected regions. A further crop-offset test on one upper effect returned 11 beats at one offset and 128 at two nearby offsets. On the other effect the left half repeatedly introduced a different kana. Those readings remain unresolved.

All five final composites reproduced exactly from their saved layers. All ten before/after cleanplates preserved every source pixel outside their erase masks, and all 2,015 manifest-listed artifacts passed their hash checks. The final renders contained 126 bound translations, of which 71 were typeset and 53 retained the printed source. Two spreadsheet translations remained available in the viewer inspector. A six-pixel search grid with five caption widths and alternate leader anchors found no nearby placement for either on the completed page without covering protected lettering or an existing edit. The cover also retained one detected credit without a translation. The final unit test run passed 1,156 tests.

## Follow-up on the upper crowd panel

Two detector polygons in the upper panel of episode 6, page 5 previously produced “Child” and “It depends on the circumstances and the people involved.” Shifting the crop boundary provided a second pair of disjoint readings that could be joined to reproduce the full reading exactly. One strip begins midway through a beat. With the literal target preserved, telling TranslateGemma which Japanese beat repeats turned its initial “sparkle” into applause. The same prompt produced crackling in a fire scene and a heartbeat in a separate control. Replaying eligibility on the five saved pages triggered this extra reading on only those two polygons.

The paint test compared three ways to erase black-outlined lettering on the same crowd. Using only pixels close to white glyph interiors recovered a spectator's silhouette, but left two Japanese characters on the panel below. The adopted mask also removes distant strokes as dark as the measured glyph outlines. This keeps more painted shadow detail while removing the lettering. Artwork underneath the original strokes remains a reconstruction.

The local comparison set is in `test-results/production-best-effort-v8/shifted-final-pages/`. The monochrome CD page was rerendered from its saved translation and had a byte-identical composite.

In the 84 × 33 pixel area containing the spectator's head and raised arms, cleanup changed 607 original pixels, down from 1,541. The two targeted phrases were the only English changes across the two painted control pages. The complete suite passed 1,157 tests. An alternate typesetting trial reduced five lines to four in the upper panel but expanded a separate two-line effect to three, so the original layout policy was retained. Some Japanese lettering outside the detected polygons is still visible.
